# Ed Rush & Killbox — Production Philosophy

**Knowledge source type:** `interview` — Valkyrie Podcast 001 (2026-08-01), ~2.8h, transcribed via faster-whisper. One of the richer real (non-template) sources in the vault.

## Core Philosophy

Constraint drives creativity; the process of getting from A to B is where the actual creative work happens, and skipping straight to a "done" sound (via a do-everything plugin, or an AI-generated result) skips the discoveries that come from the journey.

## Decisions (actionable)

- **Deliberately small effects rack.** One main reverb, one or two delays, a couple of distortion units — kept small on purpose. An overstocked plugin folder invites tweaking-as-procrastination and outsources the sound's character to a plugin's signature instead of the producer actually working the idea. `[measured, direct quote from source]`
- **Multi-stage chains over one-shot plugins.** A distortion/sound-design chain built as EQ → distort → EQ → modulate (several small deliberate steps) is preferred over one plugin that claims to do the whole job in one step — the intermediate steps are where usable discoveries happen. `[measured]`
- **Bar for adding a new permanent tool: "this does something I genuinely couldn't do before."** Not "this is a more convenient way to do the same thing." Cited example: adopting a sidechain plugin only once it enabled a new (ring-mod-style) sidechain behavior that wasn't previously possible. `[measured]`
- **Tape-era-style commitment even in the box.** Treat mix decisions as more permanent/committed (fewer infinite-recall re-litigations) to reach a flow state where "the track mixes itself" — described as more productive than endless re-tweaking. `[estimated, extrapolated from analog-era anecdote to a general production principle]`
- **A strong concept constrains the palette usefully.** Deciding a track/EP's title or concept early ruled out entire sonic directions before a single sound was chosen — used deliberately to keep a body of work coherent. `[measured]`

## What NOT to copy

Don't read "small effects rack" as a rule to never explore new plugins — the actual rule is a high bar for adoption (must unlock new capability), not blanket minimalism for its own sake.
